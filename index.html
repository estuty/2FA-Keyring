Getting started

Set up in three steps

  1. Create your vault

    Choose a passphrase of at least 12 characters. Four or more random words work well. It encrypts everything you add, and it can't be reset, so keep it safe.

  2. Add your accounts

    When a service asks you to set up an authenticator app, give 2FA Keyring the setup key, the otpauth:// link or a picture of the QR code. You can add several at once, or import from Google Authenticator.

  3. Back up, then sign in

    Download an encrypted backup and keep it somewhere safe. After that, open the app, unlock it and click a code to copy it.

Switching apps

Moving from Google Authenticator?

Bring all your accounts across in one go. The export QR codes are read on your device and never uploaded.

Read the migration guide
  1. ExportIn Google Authenticator, open the menu and choose Transfer codes, then Export codes. It may show several QR codes.
  2. ScanTake a picture of each code and choose the pictures in 2FA Keyring under Add key → Google Authenticator. Large exports split into several codes; the app tells you if one is missing.
  3. ReviewSee every account before anything is saved. Accounts already in your vault, and types the app doesn't support, are clearly marked and skipped.
  4. Check, then removeCompare a few codes, or sign in once with each account, before you delete anything from your old app.

Backup and recovery

Your vault lives in this browser. Keep a copy.

There's no server holding a copy for you, so a backup is how you recover from a lost device or cleared browser data.

Read the backup guide
Clearing browser data deletes the vault
So does resetting the device or uninstalling the browser. An encrypted backup file brings everything back.
Private windows don't keep anything
Incognito and private windows erase the vault when they close. Use a normal window.
The app tells you when to back up
It counts changes since your last backup and reminds you after you add, edit or remove keys.
Check a backup without risk
Open a backup file to confirm it works and see what's in it. Your vault isn't changed.
A lost passphrase can't be recovered
Not by you, and not by us. Keep each service's recovery codes somewhere safe and separate.

Protected

  • A stolen backup file or browser profileWithout your passphrase, the vault can't be read.
  • A breach of the website's hostThe host stores only the public site files. Your keys and passphrase never reach it.
  • Tracking and data collectionNo accounts, cookies or analytics, and the app sends nothing once loaded.
  • Someone at your screen laterThe vault locks after 5 minutes without activity by default (1, 5 or 15 minutes, or never), and can lock as soon as you switch tabs.

Not protected

  • Tampered website codeIf the site's deployment or source were compromised, your browser would run whatever was served. Check the address is 2fa-keyring.com, or host your own copy.
  • Malware or a malicious browser extensionSoftware on your device can read the page while the vault is unlocked.
  • A weak or forgotten passphraseA weak one can be guessed from a stolen backup. A forgotten one can't be reset.
  • PhishingA code typed into a fake sign-in page can be reused within seconds. Use passkeys or security keys where accounts offer them.

Security details

What happens to your keys

Every key you add is encrypted inside your browser before anything is written to storage.

  1. You typeYour passphrase

    Never stored or sent. It exists only in memory while you unlock the vault.

  2. Key derivationPBKDF2-SHA256

    600,000 rounds with a random 16-byte salt make each passphrase guess slow.

  3. EncryptionAES-256-GCM

    A fresh random IV on every save. Any tampering makes decryption fail.

  4. StorageThis browser only

    Only the encrypted vault is saved, in this browser's local storage.

Questions

Frequently asked

Can anyone else see my keys, including whoever runs this site?

No. Your keys are encrypted in your browser and saved only on your device. The site is static files with no backend to receive them. Like any website, the host sees that your IP address loaded the files, but nothing you type or store is sent.

Is the demo safe to try if I already have a vault?

Yes. The demo uses public test keys and keeps everything in memory. It never reads or writes your saved vault, and closing it leaves no trace.

What happens if I forget my passphrase?

The vault can't be opened, by you or anyone else. That's what keeps it safe. Use each service's recovery codes to sign in again and set up two-factor afresh.

How do I move to a new computer or phone?

Download an encrypted backup from the app. On the new device, open 2FA Keyring, choose "Restore from an encrypted backup" and enter the passphrase you had when you made it. See the backup guide.

Does it work offline?

Once the app is open and unlocked, codes keep working without a connection, because they're calculated from your keys and your device's clock. Opening the app again while offline isn't guaranteed: your browser may need the network to load the page.

My codes are rejected. What's wrong?

Codes depend on your device's clock, so make sure it's set automatically. If a service uses non-default settings, check the key's algorithm, digits and period against what the service shows.

Does it work with push approvals or passkeys?

No. 2FA Keyring makes the 6 to 8 digit codes that services call "authenticator app" or TOTP codes. Tapping "Approve" in a company's own app, and passkeys, are different sign-in methods. The account setup guide explains the difference.

Is it free?

Yes. There's no account, subscription or advertising, and the code is MIT licensed.
