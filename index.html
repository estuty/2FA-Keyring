What happens to your keys

Encrypted before they're stored. Never sent anywhere.

Every key you add goes through the same path, inside your browser, before anything is written to disk.

  1. You typeYour passphrase

    It's never stored or sent anywhere. It exists only in memory while you unlock the vault.

  2. Key derivationPBKDF2-SHA256

    600,000 rounds with a random 16-byte salt slow down anyone trying to guess your passphrase.

  3. EncryptionAES-256-GCM

    Your keys are sealed with a fresh random IV on every save. Any tampering makes decryption fail.

  4. StorageThis browser only

    Only the encrypted vault is saved, in this browser's local storage. It never reaches a server.

No network access, enforced by your browser

The app's Content Security Policy sets connect-src 'none', so your browser refuses any attempt by the page to send data anywhere, even if someone slipped bad code into it. All code and fonts come from this site, with no CDNs, trackers or third-party scripts.

Honest limits

What it protects against, and what it can't

No authenticator can protect you from everything. Here's exactly where the line is.

Protected

  • A stolen backup file or browser profileWithout your passphrase, the vault is unreadable ciphertext.
  • Our servers being hackedThere are no servers. Nothing about you or your keys is stored anywhere but your device.
  • Tracking and data collectionNo accounts, cookies, analytics or network requests from the app.
  • Someone walking up to your screen laterAuto-lock clears the decrypted keys from memory after inactivity, or as soon as you switch tabs.
  • Being loaded inside another siteThe app refuses to run inside a frame, so it can't be overlaid to trick your clicks.

Not protected

  • A weak passphraseEncryption is only as strong as your passphrase. The app requires 12 or more characters and rejects easy ones.
  • Malware or a malicious browser extensionSoftware on your device can read the page while it's unlocked, as with any password manager.
  • A forgotten passphraseThere's no reset and no back door. Keep your services' recovery codes somewhere safe.
  • Clearing your browser dataThat deletes the vault. Export an encrypted backup after adding keys.

Getting started

Set up in under two minutes

  1. Create your vault

    Choose a passphrase of at least 12 characters. Four or more random words work well. It encrypts everything that follows.

  2. Add your keys

    When a service shows a QR code for an authenticator app, paste the setup key or the otpauth:// link, or upload a screenshot of the QR code. You can add several at once.

  3. Back up, then sign in

    Export an encrypted backup and store it somewhere safe. From then on, open the app, unlock it and click a code to copy it.

Questions

Frequently asked

Can anyone else see my keys, including the people who run this site?

No. Your keys are encrypted in your browser and saved only on your device. This site has no backend to send them to, and the app's security policy blocks network connections. The site is just static files.

What happens if I forget my passphrase?

The vault can't be opened, by you or by anyone else. That's what keeps it secure. Use each service's recovery codes to sign in and set up two-factor again, then create a new vault.

How do I move my keys to a new computer or phone?

In the app, open Backup and save the encrypted backup file. On the new device, open the app, choose "Restore from an encrypted backup", and enter the passphrase the backup was made with.

Why does my vault disappear in a private window?

Private windows erase local storage when they close, and each browser keeps its own vault. Use a normal window, and keep a backup.

My codes are rejected. What's wrong?

Time-based codes depend on your device's clock. Make sure your clock is set automatically. If a service uses non-default settings, check the key's Advanced options (algorithm, digits, period) against what the service shows.

How can I check these claims myself?

Open your browser's developer tools on the app and watch the Network tab while you use it: you'll see only this site's own files. The full source is on GitHub, and the security page lists step-by-step checks.

Is it free?

Yes. There's no account, no subscription and no ads.
